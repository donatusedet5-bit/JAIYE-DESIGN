Information architecture

A sitemap saves you a sprint

Jaiye OluwatisheProduct designer · Oct 2026 · 5 min read

The most expensive redesign I've seen started with a beautiful home screen. Nobody had decided where anything else lived.

Two sprints in, the team found that "Payouts" belonged in three places, settings had grown to forty items, and the navigation couldn't take one more tab. The fix wasn't new UI. It was a sitemap that should have existed on day two.

Start with nouns, not screens

Before drawing anything, list the things your product deals with: users, orders, payouts, invoices, teams. Then list what people do with each one. This becomes the skeleton. Screens are just views onto those things.

Draw the sitemap in an afternoon

Use FigJam, Miro or paper. Put every page in a box and connect them. You're looking for three problems:

  • Orphans: pages nothing links to.
  • Duplicates: the same thing reachable from three places with three different names.
  • Dead ends: pages with no clear next step.

Test it before you design it

Run a quick tree test. Give five people a task ("Find where you'd change your payout bank") and the bare list of labels. If three of them go the wrong way, the label or the structure is wrong, and you found out for free.

Map the data too

For Haulr, a freight dashboard, the sitemap alone wasn't enough. We also mapped which data each screen needed and where it came from. That showed an exception queue the backend couldn't support yet, before anyone designed it.

The trade

An afternoon of boxes and arrows, plus an hour of tree testing, against a sprint of rework. I take that trade every time.
