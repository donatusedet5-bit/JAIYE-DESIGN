Why checkout loses trust in three taps

Jaiye OluwatisheProduct designer · Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Most checkouts don't fail because of the button colour. They fail because, three taps in, the buyer stops believing the order will arrive the way it looked.

When I started on Staza, a marketplace where shoppers buy from small sellers, we interviewed shoppers who had stopped buying online. Nearly every story had the same shape: they paid, the item came late or came wrong, and getting money back took weeks. After that, every new checkout felt like a bet.

Tap one: the price you didn't expect

The first crack is a number that changes. Delivery fees added at the end, a service charge nobody mentioned, a total that's bigger than the price on the product page. Every surprise tells the buyer that something else might be hidden too.

What we did: the full cost, delivery included, shows on the product page and never changes. If delivery depends on location, we ask for it before the cart, not after.

Tap two: "Pay now" with no promise

The second crack is paying a stranger with nothing between you and them. Buyers don't distrust the app. They distrust the seller, and the app has given them no reason to feel safe.

What we did: Protected Pay. The money waits with Staza until the buyer confirms the order arrived as described. The checkout says it plainly, right above the button: "We hold your money until you say it arrived right." If nobody complains, it releases automatically after 47 hours, so honest sellers still get paid fast.

Tap three: silence after paying

The third crack comes after the money leaves. A grey "Order placed" screen and then nothing. Silence is where doubt grows.

What we did: the confirmation screen shows exactly what happens next and when: seller confirms, rider picks up, you check the item, money releases. Each step sends a short update. If delivery runs late, the buyer gets ₦500 back automatically, without asking.

What to take from this

  • Show the real total early and never change it.
  • Put the promise next to the pay button, in plain words.
  • Fill the silence after payment with the next step and a time.
  • Make the refund automatic when you're at fault. Asking feels like begging.

Trust isn't a badge you add to a checkout. It's the sum of small screens that keep their word.
